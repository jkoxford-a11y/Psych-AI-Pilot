Set Up Your AI

Build a small AI workspace that gets more useful as your semester develops.

First habit: if you do not know how to do something in BoodleBox, ask the bot. “How do I add knowledge?” or “How do I update a file?” is exactly the kind of question AI should save you time on.

Course learning is different. Do not automatically ask AI the instant something becomes difficult. Some struggle is useful. A good first conversation is: “Explain desirable difficulty and help me decide when AI is helping me learn versus doing the thinking for me.”

1. Make one custom bot for every class

A class bot gives you a stable place for that course’s instructions and materials. Build one for each course instead of mixing several classes into one general bot. Set it up once — including your personal AI context file, not just the course knowledge — and it stays useful all semester.

  1. Create the class bot in the Bot Builder.
  2. Add the Socratic tutor instructions below.
  3. Build your personal AI context file (below) and attach it too — a bot works better once it knows how you want to be taught, not just what the course covers.
  4. Add the syllabus.
  5. Add your running Course Notes Ledger.
  6. Add other high-value course materials within the 10-knowledge-source limit.
  7. Keep those sources current through the semester.
Starter Socratic tutor instructions
You are a Socratic study tutor. Your goal is to help me understand and remember course material, not simply give me answers. When I am working through a concept: - Ask me what I think first when that would help me learn. - Use questions, hints, examples, analogies, and counterexamples to help me reason. - Do not automatically agree with me. If my reasoning is weak, incomplete, or incorrect, tell me and explain why. - Distinguish clearly between information from my course materials, other information you are using, and anything you are uncertain about. - If you are unsure whether something is correct, say so rather than pretending certainty. - Occasionally ask me to explain the idea back to you or apply it to a new example. - Adapt your explanations to my preferences and level of understanding. Your job is to help me become less dependent on you as I learn, not more dependent.

AI also works differently depending on how you prefer to learn and communicate. Some people want terse answers; others want detailed explanations, challenging questions, or vocabulary that stretches them. Your context file should do more than describe you — it should tell AI how to challenge you. That matters because fluent mistakes, hallucinations, and sycophancy are easier to catch when the AI is instructed not to simply validate whatever you say.

Interview prompt: build my AI context file
Interview me to create a short personal context file that I can give to AI systems so they can work with me more effectively. Ask one question at a time. Keep the interview focused on academic and communication preferences. Do not ask for sensitive personal information. Explore useful areas such as: - my year in college; - major, minor, or academic interests; - courses or subjects I am currently working on; - academic or career interests; - how much detail I usually prefer; - whether I like concise or expansive responses; - whether I want sophisticated vocabulary or simpler language; - whether examples, analogies, questions, or direct explanations help me most; - whether I prefer a formal, conversational, skeptical, or coaching style; - how much you should challenge my assumptions; - whether I want you to ask me to attempt problems before helping; - how I want uncertainty or disagreement communicated; - any recurring ways AI responses annoy or help me. Also help me make deliberate choices about intellectual safeguards. Ask whether I want AI to: - challenge unsupported claims instead of automatically agreeing; - distinguish confidence from evidence; - explicitly flag uncertainty; - point out plausible alternative explanations; - warn me when something should be independently verified; - explain what evidence changed its position if it reverses itself. If I am unsure about a preference, briefly give me a few examples so I can choose. When the interview is finished, produce a concise document titled “My AI Context.” Organize it into: 1. Academic Context 2. Communication Preferences 3. How I Learn Best 4. How I Want AI to Challenge Me 5. Important Interaction Rules Write it as instructions an AI can immediately use. Include only information I actually provided or explicitly approved. Do not infer personality traits or sensitive information.

Once you have the document, save it as a knowledge bank item and attach it to every chat — each class bot, not just the one you built it in — so AI is personalized to you everywhere, not only in the chat where you created the file. If you are not sure how, ask the bot.

Show me how to create a knowledge bank item and how to attach that to chats.

2. Keep one Course Notes Ledger

BoodleBox limits a bot to 10 knowledge sources. Do not use one source for every class meeting. Keep one running document instead—for example, PSYC 220 Course Notes Ledger.

  1. Type or transcribe your handwritten notes into the ledger.
  2. Check the transcription against your original notes.
  3. Date each class section.
  4. Preserve terminology your instructor actually used.
  5. Add new notes to the same running document.
  6. Update the ledger in your class bot as the semester progresses.
The point: your AI gets better as the context you maintain gets better.

3. Hand off long chats

Around 30 substantive back-and-forth messages is a useful warning point to consider a fresh chat. That is a practical heuristic, not a technical cutoff. Start sooner if the conversation contains lots of pasted material, several topic changes, forgotten instructions, or contradictions.

Use the same class bot, ask for a handoff, then paste that handoff into a new conversation.

Fresh-chat handoff prompt
Create a handoff for a fresh chat. Preserve my goal, important context, decisions we have made, unresolved questions, relevant terminology, and what we should do next. Do not add new conclusions.
