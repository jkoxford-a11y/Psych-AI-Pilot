Responsible Use

A few habits matter more than a long rulebook.

Learning vs. offloading

Some struggle is the mechanism that builds the skill. If attempting the problem, retrieving the idea, or constructing the explanation is what you are supposed to learn, do that part before handing it to AI.

Habit: use AI to support the struggle, not automatically remove it.

Verification

AI output is generated, not automatically verified. Check claims in proportion to their consequence. A low-stakes wording suggestion needs less checking than a citation, health claim, statistical interpretation, or factual claim you will submit in academic work.

Habit: fluency and confidence are not evidence.

Privacy

Do not put sensitive personal, medical, clinical, financial, password/account, or identifiable client or research-participant information into a course bot.

Habit: if the information would be harmful if exposed or is not yours to share, do not upload it.

Academic integrity

AI rules can differ by course and assignment. Follow the instructor’s policy and any disclosure or process requirements. You remain responsible for work submitted under your name.

Habit: when the rules are unclear, ask before submitting.

Environmental concerns

AI use has real computing and energy costs. The exact impact varies by model, provider, hardware, and data center, so simple universal numbers are usually misleading.

Habit: use the simplest model that reliably does the job, plan before repeatedly regenerating, and do not confuse easy access with zero resource cost.

Choose the model for the task

Fast, smaller models can be efficient for simple tasks. For difficult reasoning or information where mistakes matter, use a stronger model. Stronger models can still be wrong.

Habit: match capability to the task and verify important claims regardless of model.
