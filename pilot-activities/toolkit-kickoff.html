The Concept

Students get a BoodleBox setup link before the first class meeting, so account activation doesn’t eat class time. In class, you introduce it in a few minutes and assign the Student AI Toolkit as due within the week. Instead of just reading about AI literacy, students experience it directly: a four-step checklist covering the basics, setup, four hands-on experiments, and responsible use, with short Check-In prompts along the way that they record in a downloadable doc and turn in.

Psychology objectiveFirst-contact calibration — students form an initial, evidence-based sense of what AI does and doesn’t do well, before habits (good or bad) set in for the semester.
AI competencyAll four Student AI Toolkit competencies: context, sycophancy, model differences, and verification — introduced in the basics/setup steps, then tested directly in the experiments step.
TierBasic AI Literacy (Week 1)
TimeDue within one week of assignment; ~60–90 min of active work, spread out however the student likes.
Human-only componentThe short answers recorded in the AI Toolkit Check-In doc as students go — not something to hand to AI to write.
Verification requirementThe “Catch It Being Wrong” experiment requires checking one AI claim against an actual source; the rest are graded on genuine engagement and evidence of completion, not factual accuracy.

Before Class

Send students the Getting Started with BoodleBox link ahead of the first class meeting, so account activation and first-bot setup happen before day one, not during it.

In Class

Two or three minutes is enough: why the pilot exists, that this first assignment is low-stakes and about building the habit, and that it’s due within the week using the toolkit below.

Send Students the Toolkit

The actual assignment students complete lives on the student-only guide site, not this page — that keeps students from ever landing on faculty-facing navigation:

jkoxford-a11y.github.io/boodlebox-guide/toolkit.html
A four-step checklist (basics, setup, experiments, responsible use) ending with a downloadable AI Toolkit Check-In doc students fill in as they go and submit wherever you collect work.

What it covers, for your own reference:

  1. Learn the four things to know about AI. Context, accuracy, sycophancy, and model differences.
  2. Set up BoodleBox. A personal AI context file, one Course Notes Ledger per class, and one tutored class bot — attaching the syllabus and Socratic tutor instructions.
  3. Try four experiments. Convince It of a Lie (sycophancy), Find the Model You Work Well With (model differences), Catch It Being Wrong (verification), and Talk About Your Own Learning (offloading vs. learning).
  4. Use AI responsibly. Learning vs. offloading, verification, privacy, academic integrity, model choice, and environmental concerns.

Debrief Question

If you collect the Check-In doc, its “Talk About Your Own Learning” item doubles as a natural debrief prompt — students comparing answers can see they found different experiments most revealing, which is itself evidence that model behavior and student expectations both vary.
