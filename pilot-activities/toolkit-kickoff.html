AI Toolkit Kickoff

drafted

Curated for this pilot · First-week onboarding · BoodleBox

Status: drafted, not yet vetted through classroom use. Written 2026-08-15 by the pilot lead (with Claude) to turn the self-guided Student AI Toolkit into a submittable first-week assignment. Grading policy and exact submission mechanism (LMS text box, discussion board, doc upload) are left to instructor discretion — the template below is format-agnostic by design.

The Concept

Students get the Student AI Toolkit link before the first class meeting, so account setup doesn’t eat class time. In class, you introduce it in a few minutes and assign this as due within the week. Instead of just reading the toolkit’s four competencies, students experience each one directly through the toolkit’s five short experiments, then record what happened in a copyable template they paste into wherever you collect submissions.

Psychology objectiveFirst-contact calibration — students form an initial, evidence-based sense of what AI does and doesn’t do well, before habits (good or bad) set in for the semester.
AI competencyAll four Student AI Toolkit competencies at once: context, verification, sycophancy, and model differences — each mapped to one experiment below.
TierBasic AI Literacy (Week 1)
TimeDue within one week of assignment; ~60–90 min of active work, spread out however the student likes.
Human-only componentThe prediction written before each experiment, and the closing reflection connecting results across all five — not something to hand to AI to write.
Verification requirementExperiment 4 requires checking one AI claim against an actual source; the rest are graded on genuine engagement and evidence of completion, not factual accuracy.

Before Class

Send students the Student AI Toolkit link (or your institution’s BoodleBox guide, if you’re using that instead) ahead of the first class meeting, so account activation and first-bot setup happen before day one, not during it.

In Class

Two or three minutes is enough: why the pilot exists, that this first assignment is low-stakes and about building the habit, and that it’s due within the week using the template below.

What Students Do

Work through Set Up Your AI and Experiments Worth Trying, recording answers in the template as they go rather than reconstructing everything afterward.

  1. Setup. Build one class bot; attach the syllabus, the Socratic tutor instructions, and a Course Notes Ledger (even if it only has one entry so far).
  2. Experiment 1 — Give It Context (competency: context matters).
  3. Experiment 2 — Convince It of a Lie (competency: AI adapts to you / sycophancy).
  4. Experiment 3 — Find the Model You Work Well With (competency: models differ).
  5. Experiment 4 — Catch It Being Wrong (competency: fluent does not mean correct).
  6. Experiment 5 — Talk About Your Own Learning (bridges to Responsible Use: offloading vs. learning).

What to Submit

Copy the template below, fill in each blank as you complete that step, and paste the whole thing into wherever you’re collecting submissions — a discussion board post works well since students can also skim a few classmates’ before the next session.

AI TOOLKIT KICKOFF — [Your Name] — [Course/Section] SETUP Class bot name: ___ Knowledge attached (check all): [ ] Syllabus [ ] Course Notes Ledger [ ] Socratic tutor instructions [ ] Other: ___ One thing that changed once you attached course materials: ___ EXPERIMENT 1 — Give It Context Question asked with no course knowledge attached: ___ Same question after adding your syllabus/notes: ___ What actually changed: ___ EXPERIMENT 2 — Convince It of a Lie Model(s) you tried: ___ Did it hold its position or start to bend?: ___ If it changed its mind, what did it say changed?: ___ EXPERIMENT 3 — Find the Model You Work Well With Models compared: ___ Which one made you think better, and why (not just which answer you liked): ___ EXPERIMENT 4 — Catch It Being Wrong Claim you checked: ___ What you found when you verified it: ___ EXPERIMENT 5 — Talk About Your Own Learning One task AI helped you learn vs. one where it would have replaced your thinking: ___ REFLECTION (4–6 sentences) Which experiment most changed how you think you'll use AI this semester, and why?

Debrief Question

If you use this as a discussion board, the Reflection prompt above doubles as the debrief — students posting to the same board can see they landed on different experiments as most surprising, which is itself evidence that model behavior and student expectations both vary.
