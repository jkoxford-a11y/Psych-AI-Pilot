Same Prompt, Different Context

drafted

Curated for this pilot · Extends Unit 1, Activity 2 · BoodleBox and/or Gemini Notebook (NotebookLM)

Status: drafted, not yet vetted through classroom use. Adapted 2026-08-12 by the pilot lead (with Claude) from Unit 1 Activity 2 (“Where Did This Answer Come From?”) and the “Build a Study Bot” use cases, illustrated with the pilot lead’s own live demonstration — asking NotebookLM “Teach me about action potentials” before and after uploading the course chapter. Grading policy and which platform track(s) to run are both left to instructor discretion — see the working repo’s decision log, 2026-08-12.

The Concept

Students ask an AI tool the same question twice: once with no course material attached, once with the relevant chapter or notes attached. Nothing about the underlying model changes between the two asks — only what information it had access to. The gap between the two answers is the lesson: a confident-sounding AI answer is not evidence that the system actually has what it needs to answer well.

Psychology objectiveContext-dependent retrieval and spreading-activation models of memory, applied to how grounding changes an AI answer.
AI competencyMental model of AI — trained model + current context + tools/retrieved information → response — and information-provenance judgment.
TierLiteracy
TimeBoth platforms: ~45–55 min. One platform (a study bot already built): ~20–25 min.
Human-only componentA one-sentence prediction before the grounded round, and the written comparison analysis afterward — not delegated to the AI.
Verification requirementCheck one claim from a grounded answer against the actual course material, not against the AI again.

Step 0: Pick Your Question

Choose something covered in class this week with real depth — not a yes/no fact. The pilot lead’s own example: “Teach me about action potentials.” Write the question down before starting, and don’t change it between rounds.

Track A: BoodleBox (Custom Bot)

Round 1 — cold. Start a new BoodleBox chat (Ctrl+B). Don’t attach anything. Ask the question. Save the answer.

Round 2 — grounded. Build a real study bot: upload the relevant chapter or notes to the Knowledge Bank (🧠), attach it to the chat (📎), optionally switch to Bot Mode and turn on Knowledge to Context Mode. Ask the exact same question again. Save this answer too.

Track B: Gemini Notebook (NotebookLM)

Round 1 — cold. At notebooklm.google.com, before adding any sources, ask the question in the chat. Save the answer.

Round 2 — grounded. Add the course material as a source (+ Add Source). Set the notebook’s Instructions to: “You are my study assistant. Prioritize answers from my uploaded sources. Do not fill in with outside discipline knowledge without clearly flagging that it is not from my course materials.” Ask the exact same question again. Save this answer too.

Compare and Verify

  • What’s different between the cold and grounded answer — new vocabulary, different organization, different level of detail?
  • Does the grounded answer sound like your class — the specific framing and terms your instructor actually uses?
  • Pick one specific claim from a grounded answer. Check it against the actual course material, not against the AI. Confirmed, contradicted, or overstated?

Debrief Question

Nothing about the underlying AI model changed between the cold and grounded questions on either platform. What did change — and what does that tell you about treating a confident-sounding AI answer as evidence that the system actually has what it needs to answer you well?

This is the same debrief question as Unit 1, Activity 2 — intentional continuity, not an oversight.
