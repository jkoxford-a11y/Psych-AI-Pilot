Activities & Assignments

A resource hub, not just an archive — browse ready-to-use classroom activities by course. Everything marked final is drop-in ready and vetted; drafted means fully written but not yet vetted or adopted for the pilot; idea means named but not yet designed or written.

Before you browse: two kinds of evidence

Use open work where AI can be used productively, and protected work where you can establish what a student actually can do. Not every activity below needs to be protected — every important course outcome needs enough protected evidence to support the grade. See Assessment Hardening for the AI Era for the full menu of protected-assessment formats, common failure modes, and how to convert an assignment you already run.

The Analogy Stress Test

Literacy / Fluency

Two AI-generated analogies for the same concept, compared and stress-tested, then applied to a new example without the analogy to check whether real transfer occurred.

Reviewer #2: Audit the Critic

Fluency / Proficiency

AI plays a demanding peer reviewer; students classify each criticism as valid, partly valid, unsupported, mistaken, or out of scope, and justify what they'll actually change.

The Cognitive Sandwich

Literacy / Fluency

Think first, use AI for one named function, then revise independently — a before/after change audit shows exactly where outside input altered the student's thinking.

The Simulated Client Interview

Proficiency

Iterative questioning of a simulated client, compared against an instructor-supplied case file — no real client data involved.

The Statistical Code Audit

Fluency / Proficiency

Students audit AI-generated statistical code against the research question and design — not just whether it runs.

The Evidence Match Audit

Literacy

Students classify AI-generated claims by type and check whether the evidence matches their specific wording, not just their topic.
