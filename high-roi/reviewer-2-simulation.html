Reviewer #2: Audit the Critic

drafted

High-ROI Assignment Bank · Critical Evaluation · Best for Research Methods, Social Science Writing, Independent Research, Capstone, upper-level psychology courses

Status: drafted, not yet adopted for the pilot. Rewritten by the pilot lead (with ChatGPT) 2026-08-01 from the original High-ROI Assignment Bank concept — the deliverable structure and grading approach below are new, not the original Notion text.

The Concept

AI can produce confident, detailed criticism of almost any argument or research proposal. The harder skill is deciding whether that criticism is actually warranted. Students use AI as a skeptical reviewer, then evaluate the reviewer rather than automatically obeying it.

The Prompt

"Act as a demanding peer reviewer. Identify the three most important weaknesses in this argument/study. Classify each as a problem of evidence, reasoning, methodology, interpretation, or communication. Do not rewrite the work."

Student Deliverable

  • The Review: the AI's three strongest criticisms.
  • The Audit: for each criticism, classify it as:
    • Valid
    • Partly valid
    • Unsupported
    • Mistaken
    • Outside the scope of the assignment
  • The Evidence: briefly justify each verdict using the student's actual argument, data, assignment criteria, or course material.
  • The Decision: state what they will revise — and what they will deliberately leave unchanged.

Twist: students are not required to disagree with the AI. If all three criticisms are good, they should say so. Likewise, they should not accept a criticism merely because it sounds sophisticated.

Why It Works

Feedback creates a second judgment problem: before students can use criticism productively, they must determine whether it is accurate, relevant, and proportionate. AI makes this especially visible because it can generate plausible criticism even when the criticism rests on a misunderstanding of the assignment or the student's argument.

The activity therefore shifts students from "What did the AI tell me to fix?" to "What evidence would justify changing my work?"

AI Literacy Lesson

AI feedback is input, not authority. A sophisticated-sounding critique still has to earn its influence on the final product.

Psychology Connection

Metacognition, source monitoring, authority effects, confirmation bias, epistemic vigilance, and judgment under uncertainty.

Grading ROI

Very high. Faculty do not need to grade the AI review itself. The important evidence is the student's accept/modify/reject judgment and justification. A short audit table can reveal very quickly whether the student understands both the original work and the criticism.
