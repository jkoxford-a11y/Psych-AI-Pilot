The Analogy Stress Test

drafted

High-ROI Assignment Bank · Conceptual Understanding · Best for General Psychology, Cognitive Psychology, Neuroscience, Sensation & Perception, Learning

Status: drafted, not yet adopted for the pilot. Rewritten by the pilot lead (with ChatGPT) 2026-08-01 from the original High-ROI Assignment Bank concept — the deliverable structure and grading approach below are new, not the original Notion text.

The Concept

AI is exceptionally good at generating analogies that sound explanatory. But analogies work by preserving some relationships while ignoring others. Students learn to distinguish a useful model from an intuitively appealing but misleading one.

The Prompt

"Generate two substantially different analogies to explain [Concept X] to a beginning psychology student. Explain what each part of the analogy is intended to represent."

Student Deliverable

  • Before AI: briefly explain the concept in their own words and rate how well they think they understand it.
  • The Comparison: identify the important relationship(s) that both analogies are trying to represent — not just which objects correspond to which.
  • The Stress Test: choose the stronger analogy and identify:
    • what relationship it represents especially well;
    • what it oversimplifies or leaves out;
    • one false conclusion a student could draw by taking it too literally.
  • The Boundary Statement: complete: "This analogy works because ________, but it breaks down when ________."
  • The Transfer: apply the actual psychological concept to a new example without using the analogy.

Twist: the analogy that initially feels easiest to understand does not have to be the most accurate. Students can reject both AI-generated analogies if neither preserves the important structure of the concept.

Why It Works

Research on analogical learning suggests that understanding improves when learners compare cases and identify the deeper relational structure they share, rather than focusing on superficial similarities. Requiring students to explain the concept before using AI also exposes possible gaps between feeling like they understand and being able to explain a mechanism. The final transfer step tests whether the analogy helped students learn the underlying concept, rather than merely giving them a memorable metaphor.

AI Literacy Lesson

Fluency is not accuracy. AI can generate a representation that feels clarifying while quietly distorting the thing being represented.

Psychology Connection

Analogical reasoning, structure-mapping and comparison, conceptual transfer, and metacognitive calibration.

Grading ROI

High. The key products are short and easy to inspect: relational comparison, boundary statement, misconception, and transfer response. The transfer item provides a particularly clean check on whether genuine conceptual learning occurred.
