The "Fact-Checker"

proposed

High-ROI Assignment Bank · Information Literacy · Best for literature reviews, history of science

Status: proposed, not yet adopted for the pilot. Two additional workspace copies of this assignment bank exist in the source Notion with unresolved duplicate status against this original.

The Concept

AI hallucinates. This assignment weaponizes that flaw for learning.

The Prompt

"Ask AI to summarize the history/key findings of [Topic X] and include 5 citations."

Student Deliverable

The Verification: a "Truth Table" checking the AI's claims, with three columns:

  • Column A: AI claim.
  • Column B: Verified? (Yes / No / Partially)
  • Column C: Real source — citation of the actual paper that supports or refutes the claim.

Why It Works

Teaches the vital skill of verifying AI output against primary literature.

Grading ROI

High. You just check the "Real Source" column.

Implementation Rubric — "AI Auditor" Assignments

The source page proposes one standardized rubric across all six High-ROI assignments:

CriteriaPass (100%)Revise (0%)
The PromptPrompt is included and follows the specific structure (e.g., "Act as a patient…").Prompt is missing or generic ("Tell me about X").
The AuditCritique/reflection identifies specific flaws, limits, or missing info. Cites course concepts.Audit is a summary ("The AI did a good job") rather than a critique. No specific evidence cited.
The FormattingClearly labels "Student Work" vs. "AI Output."Hard to distinguish student voice from AI text.

Instructor note from the source: "Revise" does not mean fail — it means do it again until you actually critique the AI. This maintains high standards without creating a grading backlog.
