The Cognitive Sandwich: Human → AI → Human

drafted

High-ROI Assignment Bank · Metacognition & Cognitive Offloading · Best for any psychology course involving writing, interpretation, problem solving, studying, or analysis

Status: drafted, not yet adopted for the pilot. Rewritten by the pilot lead (with ChatGPT) 2026-08-01 from the original High-ROI Assignment Bank concept — the deliverable structure and grading approach below are new, not the original Notion text.

The Concept

Students often go to AI too early. Once the model supplies an answer, outline, interpretation, or explanation, it becomes difficult to know what thinking was genuinely theirs and what was imported. The Cognitive Sandwich requires students to think first, use AI for a narrow, pre-specified function — not open-ended help — and then return to independent thinking.

The Prompt

"Here is my current thinking about [Problem X]. Do not solve it for me. Identify one weakness, one missing consideration, and one alternative interpretation I should consider."

Student Deliverable

  • Human 1 — Before AI: produce a brief initial answer, interpretation, hypothesis, outline, or solution with AI closed.
  • The AI Intervention: before opening AI, name exactly which one or two functions AI is allowed to perform:
    • critique;
    • generate alternatives;
    • identify missing considerations;
    • ask probing questions;
    • explain one confusing point.
  • Human 2 — After AI: close the AI output and produce a revised response independently.
  • The Change Audit: identify, with a one-sentence reason for each:
    • one idea retained from the original;
    • one idea changed because AI exposed a weakness;
    • one AI suggestion rejected;
    • one new idea the student developed after reconsidering the problem.

Twist: students must decide what AI is for before opening it. "Help me with this" is not an acceptable use specification.

Why It Works

Generative AI makes cognitive offloading extremely easy. Offloading is not inherently bad — the problem is losing visibility into which parts of the task still require the student's own memory, reasoning, interpretation, or judgment. Requiring an initial attempt preserves evidence of the student's starting model, while the final independent pass forces the student to integrate rather than merely edit AI output.

The comparison between the first and final responses also makes metacognition concrete: students can see exactly where outside assistance changed their thinking.

The Change Audit is also a compressed version of the six-question AI Use and Evaluation Statement used elsewhere across the curriculum (see the Roadmap) — the same tool/function-chosen, accept/reject/revise, and justification structure, scoped to one activity.

AI Literacy Lesson

The important question is not simply whether to use AI, but when in the cognitive process to introduce it and what function to delegate.

Psychology Connection

Cognitive offloading, generation effects, retrieval practice, metacognition, anchoring, and desirable difficulty.

Grading ROI

Very high. Faculty can inspect the before/after change audit quickly. The assignment does not require evaluating long AI conversations; the evidence of learning is the student's initial thinking, deliberate use decision, and justified revision.
