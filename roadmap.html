Four-Year Roadmap

The full long-term vision across the entire Psychology BA — organized by student year, not calendar year, so it stays valid regardless of when a student starts. The Fall 2026 / Spring 2027 pilot is Year 1 of this plan. This has not gone through formal faculty approval — it's the direction being built toward, not a committed curriculum. See Fall 2026 Pilot for what's actually decided and running right now.

Program goal

Psychology students should graduate able to use, evaluate, and govern AI through the lens of psychological science — and to demonstrate that competence to graduate programs and employers. The curriculum moves students through four stages:

Freshman — AI-Aware
Understands AI capabilities, risks, and disclosure expectations
Sophomore — AI-Enabled
Uses AI for study support, writing, statistics, and research with verification
Junior — AI-Fluent
Evaluates AI in applied domains where bias, validity, ethics, and harm matter
Senior — AI-Proficient
Demonstrates mature, transparent, independent AI use in capstone and professional work

Program-level learning outcomes

By graduation, Psychology majors should be able to:

  1. Explain AI using psychological science — use concepts from cognition, learning, social psychology, neuroscience, development, and applied psychology to explain how humans interact with AI.
  2. Evaluate AI output scientifically — compare AI claims against course material, empirical evidence, statistical output, diagnostic criteria, measurement principles, and ethical standards.
  3. Use AI transparently — document AI use clearly, including prompts, outputs, revisions, verification methods, and human contribution.
  4. Identify AI bias and overreach — identify stereotype reproduction, invalid inference, hallucination, false certainty, cultural bias, and inappropriate recommendations.
  5. Preserve human professional judgment — explain when AI should assist, when it should be supervised, and when it should not be used.
  6. Translate AI-supported psychology skills to workforce contexts — connect psychology training to responsible AI use in research, communication, assessment, clinical-adjacent work, organizational settings, and public-facing communication.

Shared structures across all courses

AI Use and Evaluation Statement

Used on any assignment involving AI. Students answer: (1) What AI tool did you use? (2) What did you ask it to do? (3) What did it produce? (4) What did you accept, reject, or revise? (5) How did you verify the final answer? (6) What are you responsible for as the human author?

Year 1 — AI-Aware (Freshman)

Core goal: students learn that AI is useful but fallible. They practice checking AI explanations against course material and begin developing transparent AI-use habits.

Signature assignment — AI Explanation Audit: students ask AI to explain a course concept, then evaluate what was accurate, incomplete, or misleading, and how the textbook emphasized it differently. Signature portfolio artifact: AI Use Autobiography (Gen Psych, Fall) — establishes the baseline for the entire major.

Recommended assignment cadence for faculty

AI should be integrated where it strengthens course outcomes — not forced into every assignment.

Course levelRecommended minimum
Introductory courses (PSYC 101, 275W)1–3 brief AI literacy activities
Statistics and research methods2–4 AI-supported process assignments
Mid-level content courses1–2 AI fluency assignments
Upper-level applied courses1 larger AI audit or project where appropriate
Capstone (PSYC 495W)AI Methods Appendix + portfolio completion

Ethical spine of the curriculum

Ethics appears at every level — not only in PSYC 430.

Ethical issueStudent behavior
TransparencyDisclose when, how, and why AI contributed
AccuracyVerify claims against credible sources, data, or course materials
PrivacyNever enter real client, student, patient, participant, or internship information into public AI tools
BiasTest whether AI responses differ across demographic, cultural, or disability-related variables
Human judgmentTreat AI as support, not authority
Fairness and accessConsider who has access to paid tools and how assignments can avoid deepening inequity
Intellectual humilityIdentify what remains unknown after AI assistance
