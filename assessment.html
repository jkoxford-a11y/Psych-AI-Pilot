Assessment Approach

Full battery structure and item text, confirmed 2026-08-01 against the 2026-07-13 planning meeting's decisions. GLAT items adapted from Jin, Martinez-Maldonado, Gašević & Yan, arXiv:2411.00283 (CC BY 4.0). MAILS items from Koch, Carolus, Wienrich & Latoschik (2024), Heliyon.

Structure at a glance

InstrumentWhat it measuresWho / when
3A — GLAT (20 items)Objective generative-AI knowledge, multiple choiceAll respondents, start and end of semester, identical form, no AI/notes/search allowed
3B — Access & Prior-Use Questionnaire (5 items)Prior AI experience and accessAll respondents, baseline only
4 — MAILS (10 items)Self-reported AI literacy and self-efficacy, 0–10 scaleAll respondents, start and end of semester
3E — Post-Use, Access & Acceptability Questionnaire (10 items)How the platform was actually used and experiencedPilot-section students only, end of semester

A scenario-judgment task and an AI-output annotation task were considered and cut entirely — too easy to complete with AI unsupervised outside class, and too much added burden for a first-year pilot. No per-item confidence ratings. No counterbalanced forms — same instrument, same order, administered pre and post.

Who takes it

The student survey is distributed broadly — not limited to pilot-section students — specifically to create a natural comparison group against pilot participants, even though response rates from non-pilot students are expected to be lower. A separate survey goes to all faculty.

Privacy and approval

Responses are linked across time using a student ID, with the code key held separately by a neutral institutional office — researchers cannot identify individuals. Formal institutional review (PHS) approval isn't required for this internal academic-assessment use; it would only be needed if results are later published or presented, and consent is being collected upfront to preserve that option.

Portfolio evidence

Alongside the formal instruments, each participating course contributes one lightweight student artifact showing: (1) what the student used AI to do, (2) how the student checked or revised the output, (3) what psychological knowledge or human judgment the student brought to the task.

What this does and doesn't establish

This Fall's evidence speaks to whether students' AI literacy measurably improved over one semester. It does not, on its own, establish that any single assignment caused that improvement, and it doesn't yet address whether assessments across the curriculum are reliably protected from AI misuse — that remains future work.
