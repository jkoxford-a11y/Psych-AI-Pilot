Human-AI Cognitive Alignment Tools

Status: Final · Category: Protocol pack (all stages) · Last updated: 2026-07-10

Reusable Protocol Pack

These are lightweight protocol wrappers, not full turnkey assignments. Faculty can attach them to many different assignments.

How to Use This Pack

Use these protocols when a full AI assignment would be too much, but students still need structure around AI use. Each protocol can be copied into an assignment sheet, LMS page, or reflection prompt.

Not sure which protocol fits your goal? Use this table:

Faculty goalUse this protocol
Students keep using AI without thinking about what they're supposed to learnProtocol 1 — Cognitive Alignment Contract
Hard to tell whether students actually learned anything or just got AI to do itProtocol 2 — Learning Receipt
Students don't stop to consider whether AI is even the right toolProtocol 3 — AI Use Triage
Need a lightweight standing rule students can apply to any assignmentProtocol 4 — Resource Rule
Want to add structure to a full AI assignment from the databaseCombine: Contract (before) + Learning Receipt (after)
Skeptical students or faculty asking "why not just opt out?"Protocol 4 + the Efficiency Ethic assignment (docs/efficiency-ethic.md)

The four protocols are:

  1. Cognitive Alignment Contract — before AI use
  2. Learning Receipt — after AI-supported learning
  3. AI Use Triage — before deciding whether to use AI
  4. Resource Rule — standing rule for all AI-supported assignments

Protocol 1: Cognitive Alignment Contract

Purpose

Before using AI, students define the human role and the AI role. This prevents accidental outsourcing and makes the intended learning process visible.

Student Instructions

Before opening an AI tool, complete this contract.

QuestionYour answer
What is the task?
What am I supposed to learn or practice?
What cognitive work must I do myself?
What may AI help with?
What may AI not do?
What evidence will I keep?
How will I check whether I actually learned anything?

Post-Use Audit

After completing the task, answer:

  1. Did I follow my contract? Where did I drift?
  2. Did AI preserve or replace the learning target?
  3. What would I change in my contract next time?

Best Used For

  • writing assignments
  • study plans
  • research-methods projects
  • concept explanations
  • portfolio work

Protocol 2: Learning Receipt

Purpose

AI-supported learning should leave evidence that the student's understanding changed. This protocol requires before/after evidence of learning, not just a final answer.

Student Instructions

Submit the following with your assignment.

1. Before AI

Write your answer, explanation, plan, or interpretation before using AI.

2. AI Interaction

Document:

  • the prompt you used
  • the AI output or feedback
  • any AI suggestions you rejected
  • any claims you checked

3. After AI

Revise your work. Then identify three changes:

ChangeWhy did you make it?What evidence or reasoning supports it?

4. Learning Check

Close AI and answer one question from memory:

What do I understand now that I did not understand before?

Best Used For

  • concept learning
  • exam review
  • AI tutor feedback
  • research summaries
  • methods/statistics interpretation

Protocol 3: AI Use Triage

Purpose

Students should decide whether AI is appropriate before using it. This protocol asks them to weigh need, cost, risk, and learning value.

Student Instructions

Before using AI, complete the four-part triage.

DimensionQuestionYour answer
NeedIs AI actually needed, or would notes, textbook, search, peer discussion, or instructor guidance be better?
CostHow much interaction, output, or model power is likely required?
RiskWhat could go wrong: privacy, accuracy, ethics, authorship, emotional harm, or course expectations?
Learning valueWill AI preserve or replace the cognitive work I am supposed to practice?

Decision

Choose one:

  • No AI
  • Do the work myself first, then ask for feedback
  • Minimal AI support
  • Targeted AI support
  • Full AI-supported workflow with documentation

Best Used For

  • ambiguous AI-use situations
  • high-stakes assignments
  • reflective writing
  • sensitive topics
  • research or writing workflows

Protocol 4: Resource Rule

Purpose

Resource-aware AI use is part of responsible use. Students should use enough AI to support the task, but not so much that they waste output, obscure their own thinking, or avoid simpler tools.

Student-Facing Rule

Use the least AI that preserves the learning goal and accomplishes the task well.

That means:

  • Use your notes, textbook, search, or peer/instructor help when those are better tools.
  • Draft or think first when the assignment is designed to practice your own reasoning.
  • Give AI enough context to be useful, but do not paste irrelevant material.
  • Ask for targeted feedback before asking for a full rewrite.
  • Avoid repeated full regenerations when surgical edits will do.
  • Save project state or handoff notes instead of repeatedly re-explaining the same context.
  • Do not ask AI to reconstruct work you could have documented.

Disclosure Add-On

When AI is used, add one sentence to the disclosure:

I used AI in a resource-aware way by _____. I avoided unnecessary AI use by _____.

Examples:

  • I drafted my explanation first and used AI only for targeted feedback.
  • I used a handoff file instead of re-explaining my project from scratch.
  • I asked for three specific edits instead of a full rewrite.
  • I used the textbook rather than AI for the definition because accuracy mattered and the source was already assigned.

Instructor Note

Do not present this as exact carbon accounting. Present it as professional proportionality: the tool should fit the task, the risk, and the learning goal.

Combined One-Page Student Version

Use this condensed version when space is limited.

Before I Use AI

  1. What am I supposed to learn or practice?
  2. What work must I do myself first?
  3. Is AI actually the right tool?
  4. What risks matter here — privacy, accuracy, ethics, authorship, or learning?
  5. What evidence will I keep?

While I Use AI

  1. Use the smallest adequate prompt.
  2. Ask for feedback before replacement.
  3. Check important claims.
  4. Reject suggestions that are wrong, vague, or misaligned.
  5. Save decisions, constraints, and next steps if the work will continue.

After I Use AI

  1. What did AI contribute?
  2. What did I decide?
  3. What did I reject or verify?
  4. What changed in my understanding?
  5. Could I explain the key idea without AI now?
