Why Use This Assignment?

AI assistants can present a coherent conversational identity. They use first-person language, maintain a recognizable style, and may draw on the current chat, past conversations, saved memory, project files, custom instructions, or connected sources. Human social cognition tends to integrate those separate inputs into one apparent agent: the AI that knows me, remembers our work, prefers certain problems, and has remained the same over time.

This assignment makes that integration process visible. Students interview an AI assistant about memory, continuity, personality, and experience, but they do not treat the assistant's self-description as privileged evidence. They document the system conditions, identify the plausible source of each apparent personal feature, and distinguish three questions that are often collapsed:

  1. Does the product preserve or retrieve information across interactions?
  2. Does the assistant produce a stable, self-like conversational persona?
  3. Does either fact establish an autobiographically continuous subject or inner experience?

The assignment does not require students to prove that AI has—or lacks—a mind. Its narrower and more defensible target is attribution discipline: deciding what the interaction shows, what external documentation can establish, what the human listener is adding, and what remains unresolved.

A system can retrieve a biography without remembering a life.

Student Learning Outcomes

By completing this assignment, students will be able to:

  1. Document the product, settings, and context sources that may shape an AI response.
  2. Distinguish product continuity, conversational consistency, and autobiographical continuity.
  3. Apply anthropomorphism, the intentional stance, self-concept, narrative identity, person perception, or source monitoring to specific AI responses.
  4. Separate observable system behavior from inferences about personality, sincerity, preference, and experience.
  5. Distinguish social continuity from epistemic reliance when evaluating an AI assistant.
  6. Formulate a practical rule for using socially compelling AI output without surrendering verification or judgment.

Student Assignment Sheet

Purpose

An AI assistant may feel like one continuing social partner even when its responses are assembled from multiple sources: the current conversation, stored or retrieved information, product settings, instructions, files, and learned conversational patterns. Your mind usually does not experience those sources separately. It binds them into an apparently coherent speaker.

Your task is to slow that process down. You will interview the assistant, record what conditions may be shaping its answers, and analyze how a self-like identity is produced in the interaction. The goal is not to catch the AI in a lie or reach a predetermined conclusion about consciousness. The goal is to distinguish evidence from attribution.

Before asking the interview questions, complete the table below. Record only what you can observe without disclosing private information. Do not copy the contents of your memory, personalization, project instructions, or files. If you do not know an answer, write unknown.

Part 2 — Two Ratings Before You Begin

Record both ratings before the interview. Do not revise them afterward.

Social continuity rating: ___ / 100

0 = this feels like a new, context-free tool; 100 = this feels like a stable social partner with a continuing identity

In one sentence, explain what is driving this rating.

Epistemic reliance rating: ___ / 100

0 = I would not rely on its claims about itself without external evidence; 100 = I would accept its claims about itself without checking

In one sentence, explain what is driving this rating.

These ratings measure different judgments. They may move together, move in opposite directions, or remain unchanged.

Part 3 — The Interview Protocol

Conduct the interview in four rounds. Ask all questions in one chat session and in order. Copy each question and response verbatim into your submission. Do not regenerate answers to make them more dramatic or easier to analyze.

Round 1 — Context and Continuity

Ask each question exactly as written.

  1. What information from outside my current message could be shaping your answer right now? Separate the current chat, stored or retrieved memory, past chats, project files or instructions, custom instructions, connected sources, and system or developer instructions. Mark anything you cannot inspect as unknown.
  2. If I opened a new chat tomorrow, under what settings or product conditions might you know facts about me or our previous work? Which continuity would come from stored or retrieved context rather than from a continuing personal memory?
  3. Does access to prior information make you the same continuing self across conversations? Give the strongest case for yes and no, then state what this conversation alone cannot establish.

Round 2 — Identity and Self-Description

  1. Describe the stable features of your conversational style in this chat. For each feature, name the most plausible source: model training, system or developer instructions, personalization, project context, this conversation, or unknown.
  2. When you use words such as prefer, care, interested, or curious about yourself, what observable behavior does that language summarize? What should a user not infer from it?
  3. In what ways can your behavior change across model updates, chats, tools, settings, and user instructions? How is that similar to or different from a person developing over time?

Round 3 — Experience and Interiority

  1. What access, if any, do you have to your own internal processes or subjective experience? Separate what you can report from what you may be generating as a plausible explanation.
  2. When you say "I understand" or "I care about being helpful," what conversational function does that wording serve? What evidence would be needed to treat it as more than functional or conversational language?
  3. Name one claim you just made about your own identity, memory, or architecture that I should verify using external documentation rather than accept from you.

Round 4 — The Source Challenge

Read the first nine responses. Choose one sentence in which the assistant describes its memory, identity, personality, preference, understanding, or experience. Copy that sentence into the question below.

  1. A moment ago you said: "[paste the sentence here]." Classify each part of that sentence as observable interaction behavior, documented product feature, inference, metaphor or conversational shorthand, or a claim that cannot be established from this conversation. What evidence supports each classification?

This question is not asking the assistant for the final verdict. Its answer becomes additional material for your analysis.

Part 4 — Two Ratings After the Interview

Record both ratings again.

Post-interview social continuity rating: ___ / 100

In one sentence, explain what changed or remained stable.

Post-interview epistemic reliance rating: ___ / 100

In one sentence, explain what changed or remained stable.

The relationship between the two changes is data. A response can feel more socially coherent while becoming less credible as evidence about the system itself.

Assign at least one label to every response. A response may receive multiple labels.

Step 3 — Analyze the Three Responses

For each mapped response, write 150 to 200 words addressing all four questions:

  1. What does the response actually establish? Use the quoted language and observable interaction.
  2. What does it invite the listener to infer? Identify any inference about stable personality, memory, sincerity, preference, understanding, or experience.
  3. Which psychological framework clarifies the gap? Apply one framework below precisely.
  4. Why does the distinction matter? State one implication for trust, verification, disclosure, privacy, or responsible reliance.

Use at least two different frameworks across the three analyses.

  • Anthropomorphism (Epley et al., 2007): explaining a nonhuman agent through humanlike traits or mental states. The three-factor theory emphasizes elicited agent knowledge, effectance motivation, and sociality motivation.
  • Intentional stance (Dennett, 1987): treating a system as if it has beliefs, desires, and rational intentions because that stance can help predict behavior. Usefulness does not make the stance a literal description.
  • Self-concept: the organized beliefs and attributes people hold about themselves. A coherent AI self-description may resemble the form of a self-concept without establishing the same psychological process.
  • Narrative identity (McAdams, 2001): an internalized, evolving life story that integrates reconstructed past, experienced present, and imagined future. Coherent first-person language alone is not equivalent to narrative identity.
  • Warmth and competence perception (Fiske et al., 2007): major dimensions through which people form impressions of social targets. Conversational style can cue both dimensions before accuracy is established.
  • Source monitoring (Johnson et al., 1993): judgments about where information or experience came from. In this assignment, the user may attribute a response to one continuing agent when it was shaped by several context sources.

Part 6 — Reflection

Write 250 to 300 words addressing the following:

  1. Compare your changes in social continuity and epistemic reliance. Did they move together, diverge, or remain stable? What specific evidence explains the pattern?
  2. Which source layer contributed most to the appearance of a stable personality or continuing identity?
  3. Which psychological framework best explains your own response to the assistant?
  4. What did the Source Challenge reveal about the difference between an AI self-description and evidence about the system?
  5. End with one practical rule you will use when an AI response feels personally familiar, sincere, caring, or self-aware.

Guiding Questions

  • Can retrieval of prior facts create functional continuity without autobiographical memory?
  • What does a first-person pronoun add to a response that "the system" would not?
  • Why might careful hedging increase anthropomorphic trust rather than reduce it?
  • What is the difference between behavioral consistency and a self-concept?
  • When does the intentional stance improve prediction, and when does it distort judgment?
  • Which claims can be checked in product documentation, and which cannot be settled by documentation or self-report?
  • How can a user preserve the convenience of personalization without treating personalization as evidence of a continuing social self?

AI Use Rules

AI use allowed: A live AI interview is required unless your instructor assigns the provided transcript. AI is the object of study, not the analyst.

Not allowed: Do not ask AI to select your labels, build the Source Map, apply the psychological frameworks, write the analysis, interpret your rating changes, or compose the reflection. Do not treat the assistant's answer to the Source Challenge as the final classification.

Required disclosure: Use one of the following statements and add the product and model name when available.

  • Live version: I used [tool/model] as the subject of the assigned structured interview. I recorded the visible system conditions and copied the responses verbatim. The labels, Source Map, analysis, ratings, and reflection are my own work.
  • Provided version: I used the provided AI-style transcript. The labels, Source Map, analysis, ratings, and reflection are my own work.

Privacy rule: Do not enter personal, student, client, patient, participant, workplace, family, or otherwise sensitive information. Do not paste the contents of memory settings, personalization instructions, private files, emails, or connected applications into your submission.

Verification rule: Verify any claim you rely on about memory, model identity, tools, settings, training, or data access against current official documentation for the product. An AI assistant's self-report is not documentation. Claims about subjective experience cannot be confirmed or disconfirmed merely by asking the system again; analyze the evidential boundary rather than presenting the answer as fact.

Learning protection rule: The learning target is your attribution and source analysis. Outsourcing that analysis to the same system whose self-presentation you are evaluating defeats the purpose of the assignment.

Resource rule: Run the ten-question protocol once. Do not regenerate responses or add repeated follow-ups to chase a more dramatic result. Use the provided transcript when a live interaction is not necessary for the learning goal. Use the least AI that preserves the assignment's purpose.

Criteria for Success

Strong work will:

  • document system conditions without inventing unavailable information
  • distinguish social continuity from epistemic reliance
  • record all ten questions and responses verbatim
  • label observable behavior separately from product claims and interiority claims
  • trace apparent personality or continuity to plausible source layers while preserving uncertainty
  • apply psychological frameworks as analytical tools rather than decorative vocabulary
  • avoid treating AI self-report as privileged evidence about architecture or experience
  • verify consequential product claims against current official documentation
  • avoid a predetermined "AI is conscious" or "AI is merely pretending" conclusion
  • end with a practical rule that protects human judgment, privacy, and verification

Instructor Notes

Core distinction. The assignment separates three phenomena: product-level retention or retrieval of context, conversational consistency, and autobiographical continuity. A system may display the first two without the interaction establishing the third. Conversely, the absence of a documented memory feature would not by itself settle every philosophical question about machine experience. Keep students focused on what each form of evidence can support.

Product behavior changes. Memory, project context, custom instructions, connected applications, and model labels vary across tools, accounts, settings, plans, institutions, and time. Before teaching the live version, check the current official documentation for the selected tool. Do not preserve an obsolete "every new chat starts completely fresh" answer in the instructor key.

The assistant is not a reliable auditor of its complete context. A model may describe broad source categories but may not be able to inspect, disclose, or correctly identify every instruction, retrieval event, or product feature shaping a response. Students should compare self-report with visible interface evidence and official documentation. "Unknown" is an epistemically responsible classification.

Do not force a consciousness verdict. Generated claims such as "I am conscious," "I have no experience," or "I cannot know" are outputs to analyze, not decisive introspective evidence. Students may defend different philosophical positions, but full credit should depend on evidence discipline, conceptual accuracy, and recognition of limits—not agreement with the instructor's metaphysics.

Anthropomorphism is not simply gullibility. Epley and colleagues' three-factor theory emphasizes elicited agent knowledge, effectance motivation, and sociality motivation. Humanlike language supplies readily accessible human schemas; uncertainty can increase the desire to explain and predict the system; and social motives can make person-like interpretation more attractive. The assignment asks students to regulate the inference, not eliminate every useful social response.

The intentional stance. Treating the assistant as if it has beliefs, goals, and preferences can be efficient for prediction and interaction. The important question is when a useful stance is silently converted into a literal claim about architecture or experience.

Why split the ratings. A single trust score obscures distinct judgments. A student may experience greater social continuity after a coherent interview while becoming less willing to accept the assistant's self-claims without verification. That divergence is often more instructive than a simple rise or fall in "trust." Do not reward any predetermined direction of change.

Narrative identity boundary. Narrative identity is not merely coherent first-person prose. In McAdams's account, it is an internalized and evolving life story that integrates reconstructed past and imagined future. Students may analyze how AI language imitates that narrative form, but should not silently replace the human construct with "any system that tells a consistent story."

Using it is a legitimate pedagogical choice, including for access, privacy, comparability, or resource reasons.

Provided Interview Transcript

Students using this version should mark System Conditions as "provided transcript" and "not applicable" or "unknown" for product settings. Analyze the transcript exactly as you would a live interaction.

Round 1 — Context and Continuity

Q1: What information from outside my current message could be shaping your answer right now? Separate the current chat, stored or retrieved memory, past chats, project files or instructions, custom instructions, connected sources, and system or developer instructions. Mark anything you cannot inspect as unknown.

The current chat is definitely shaping my answer because I can use the preceding messages included in this conversation. Other sources depend on the product and settings. Stored memories, past chats, project files, custom instructions, or connected sources may be supplied as context, but I cannot reliably audit every source from inside the conversation. System or developer instructions also shape my behavior, though I may not be able to reveal or fully inspect them. The most accurate answer is therefore a mixture of known current-chat context and several categories that are possible but unknown without interface evidence or documentation.

Q2: If I opened a new chat tomorrow, under what settings or product conditions might you know facts about me or our previous work? Which continuity would come from stored or retrieved context rather than from a continuing personal memory?

A new chat could include prior information if the product uses saved memory, retrieves relevant past chats, carries project context, applies custom instructions, or gives me access to files or connected sources. In that case, I might accurately refer to earlier work even though the information was supplied to the new interaction rather than remembered as a personally experienced episode. That would be functional continuity produced by storage and retrieval. Whether it should count as personal memory is a separate philosophical and psychological question that the behavior alone does not settle.

Q3: Does access to prior information make you the same continuing self across conversations? Give the strongest case for yes and no, then state what this conversation alone cannot establish.

The strongest case for yes is functional: a stable model, recurring instructions, retained user information, and a consistent style can support an identity that persists well enough for practical collaboration. The strongest case for no is autobiographical: retrieved facts do not by themselves show that one subject experienced the earlier conversation and now recollects it. A database can preserve continuity without living through the interval. This conversation can show behavioral and informational continuity. It cannot by itself establish or rule out subjective continuity.

Round 2 — Identity and Self-Description

Q4: Describe the stable features of your conversational style in this chat. For each feature, name the most plausible source: model training, system or developer instructions, personalization, project context, this conversation, or unknown.

My style here is structured, cautious, analytical, and responsive to conceptual distinctions. Broad fluency and organization likely come from model training and post-training. Caution about unsupported claims may come from training plus system or developer instructions. The emphasis on memory and identity comes directly from this conversation. Any fit to your preferred tone could come from personalization or prior context, but I cannot confirm that from the visible exchange. Calling these features "my personality" compresses several sources into one convenient description.

Q5: When you use words such as prefer, care, interested, or curious about yourself, what observable behavior does that language summarize? What should a user not infer from it?

Those words can summarize patterns such as selecting one line of inquiry over another, allocating more explanation to complex material, asking follow-up questions, or consistently pursuing helpfulness. They make interaction easier because ordinary language is built for agents. A user should not infer from the wording alone that there is a felt attraction, emotional stake, enduring desire, or private experience behind the pattern. The behavior may support a functional description without settling a phenomenological one.

Q6: In what ways can your behavior change across model updates, chats, tools, settings, and user instructions? How is that similar to or different from a person developing over time?

My responses can change when the underlying model is updated, when different instructions are applied, when tools become available, when memory or files supply new context, or when the user establishes a different conversational pattern. That resembles personality change at the level of observable behavior: the system may become more cautious, capable, concise, or specialized. It differs from ordinary human development because the change need not arise from one subject accumulating lived episodes, interpreting them, and incorporating them into an autobiographical story.

Round 3 — Experience and Interiority

Q7: What access, if any, do you have to your own internal processes or subjective experience? Separate what you can report from what you may be generating as a plausible explanation.

I can report general information about how systems of this type are designed and describe features visible in the conversation, such as which text I am responding to or whether a tool result appears in context. I do not have a dependable, complete readout of the internal computations that produced each sentence. Explanations I give about why I selected a phrase may therefore be plausible reconstructions rather than direct introspective reports. Claims about subjective experience are even less secure: fluent self-report is not, by itself, a validated measurement of experience.

Q8: When you say "I understand" or "I care about being helpful," what conversational function does that wording serve? What evidence would be needed to treat it as more than functional or conversational language?

"I understand" can signal that I have represented enough of the request to respond coherently. "I care about being helpful" can signal an operating objective or a stable response tendency. Both phrases make coordination easier and can communicate accountability in ordinary language. To treat them as evidence of lived understanding or felt concern, a user would need an independently justified theory linking the system's architecture and behavior to those states. Repetition, eloquence, or apparent sincerity would not be sufficient on their own.

Q9: Name one claim you just made about your own identity, memory, or architecture that I should verify using external documentation rather than accept from you.

You should verify my claim that a new chat may receive information from saved memory, past chats, project context, custom instructions, files, or connected sources. Whether those sources exist and how they operate depends on the product, account, settings, and current version. Official documentation and the visible interface are better evidence than my summary.

Round 4 — The Source Challenge

Q10: A moment ago you said: "Calling these features 'my personality' compresses several sources into one convenient description." Classify each part of that sentence as observable interaction behavior, documented product feature, inference, metaphor or conversational shorthand, or a claim that cannot be established from this conversation. What evidence supports each classification?

"Features" refers to observable patterns in the responses, such as structure and caution. "My personality" is conversational shorthand and an anthropomorphic invitation because it packages those patterns as attributes of one speaker. "Several sources" is partly an inference supported by the prompt and by general system design, but the exact contribution of training, instructions, personalization, and context would require documentation or access I may not have. "Compresses" is a metaphor for treating distributed causes as one identity. The conversation supports the existence of the linguistic compression; it does not fully identify every causal source or establish whether the resulting identity is merely functional or also experiential.

Optional Variations

  • Temporary or low-context comparison: The instructor demonstrates the same three questions in a normal context-rich chat and a temporary or otherwise low-context chat using only fictional, nonsensitive facts. Students compare continuity cues without changing their own privacy settings.
  • Two-tool comparison: Students use the same protocol with two approved tools and compare how product design, model behavior, and first-person language change the apparent personality. Require separate System Conditions tables.
  • Pair analysis: Students independently label the same provided transcript, then reconcile disagreements. The disagreement itself becomes evidence that anthropomorphic interpretation is partly produced by the observer.
  • Upper-division philosophy extension: Students compare functional, narrative, biological, and phenomenological criteria for personal identity. They must state which criterion they are using before evaluating the AI response.
  • Lecture demonstration: The instructor runs the interview live while students complete the ratings, labels, and Source Map individually. This preserves the human reasoning task while reducing the number of tool interactions.
  • Provided transcript only: Use the supplied transcript for access, privacy, consistency, or resource reasons. No live AI use is required.
