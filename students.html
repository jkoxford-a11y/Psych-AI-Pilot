Assigning the Student Toolkit

The toolkit gets students into BoodleBox, gives them honest numbers on what AI use costs the environment, and builds the fundamental AI literacy every beginner needs. This page is your briefing on it — what it asks of students, and how to assign it.

Open the Student AI Toolkit →
Part of the BoodleBox student guide site — students work through it without ever seeing this faculty site.

The toolkit is self-guided and written for students who have never used AI, built around doing rather than reading. To give them a reason to finish it, assign the AI Toolkit Check-In (Word doc) alongside it: five short prompts spread through the toolkit, asking what students observed rather than whether they got a right answer. They fill it in as they go and submit it wherever you collect work.

What it covers

1. Using AI Well

Four concepts — context, fluency versus correctness, sycophancy, and model differences — each with a naive-versus-competent contrast.

2. Set Up Your AI

A personal AI context file, a running course-notes ledger, and one Socratic tutor bot per class built from both. Includes handoff practice for long chats.

3. Experiments Worth Trying

Four hands-on tests: talk a model into a false claim, compare two models on a task from their major, check an answer with and without retrieval, and reflect on their own cognitive offloading.

4. Responsible Use

Learning versus offloading, verification, privacy, academic integrity, model choice, and environmental cost — as habits rather than a rulebook.

If a student gets stuck on BoodleBox itself rather than on the ideas, point them to the optional BoodleBox Technical Reference — Knowledge Bank versus bot knowledge, model switching, chat sharing, prompt modes. It is deliberately outside the required path.
