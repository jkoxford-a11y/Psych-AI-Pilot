Student AI Toolkit

The toolkit students actually use is published on the student guide site. This page tells you what it asks of them and links you to it.

Open the Student AI Toolkit →
Part of the BoodleBox student guide site, which students see without this site’s faculty navigation.

The toolkit is self-guided and framed for students, not faculty. It assumes no prior AI experience and is built around doing rather than reading — students build a working setup and run experiments on it. Steps 1, 3, and 4 are worked through once; step 2 includes a course-notes ledger students maintain all semester.

What it covers

1. Using AI Well

Four concepts: context, fluency versus correctness, sycophancy, and model differences. Each with a naive-versus-competent contrast.

2. Set Up Your AI

A personal AI context file, a running course-notes ledger, and one Socratic tutor bot per class built from both. Includes handoff practice for long chats.

3. Experiments Worth Trying

Students try to talk a model into a false claim, compare models on a task from their major, test an answer with and without retrieval, and discuss their own cognitive offloading.

4. Responsible Use

Learning versus offloading, verification, privacy, academic integrity, model choice, and environmental cost — as habits rather than a rulebook.

If you assign it

The toolkit carries five CHECK-IN prompts telling students what to record. They collect these in the AI Toolkit Check-In (Word doc) and submit it where you direct. Short answers are expected; the prompts ask what students observed, not whether they got a right answer.

There is also an optional BoodleBox Technical Reference covering platform mechanics — Knowledge Bank versus bot knowledge, model switching, chat sharing permissions, prompt modes. It is deliberately not part of the required path; point students to it when they get stuck on the platform rather than the ideas.

Why it lives there

This site previously carried its own copy of the toolkit. That copy was retired on 15 August 2026 because maintaining two versions meant they drifted apart, and the stale one was the copy faculty saw. The student guide site is now the single source; earlier versions remain in this repository’s git history.
